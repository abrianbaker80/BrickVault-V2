# Inventory, database and BrickLink contract

D01, D02, D05 and D09 establish the intended behavior. Provider and production qualification require separate evidence and authorization. [Master plan](MASTER_PLAN.md) · [Coverage](FEATURE_MATRIX.md) · [Decision register](DECISIONS.md)

## Whose fact is it?

The owner counts pieces, chooses their condition, storage and sale terms, and hands parcels to carriers. V2 records those decisions. BrickLink originates sale and order facts and reports its own listings. A sale may already have reduced the remote quantity before V2 hears about it. Treating a remote difference as a replenishment instruction can sell the same pieces twice.

### Units and identities

Use integer pieces. A catalog key is catalog namespace + item type + item number, preserving leading zeroes, variants and item-type boundaries. Map colors with typed source IDs, retaining explicit not-applicable separately from unknown. A sellable variant adds color, new/used condition, applicable set completeness, packaging/instructions and customer-visible grade/description where these change the promise. A bin label is a physical address. A receipt layer identifies a particular source, batch, currency and acquisition cost. A position is a layer × variant × location × stock state. One bin may hold multiple layers. One layer may occupy several bins. Mixing pieces physically can require a documented cost-flow convention.

A local offer owns an explicit slice of compatible stock, its seller-defined terms, unit price/currency, bulk multiple, tier/discount schedule, retain/stockroom attributes, and descriptions. Keep a stable V2 offer ID even when a BrickLink inventory ID changes. Keep dated bindings (store, remote inventory ID, offer, effective interval, verified evidence); enforce at most one current binding for each end. Similar catalog keys can identify several genuine BrickLink lots. Different descriptions, grades, conditions, retention or deal terms can justify separate offers. Allocate units to one offer only. Default to one offer per homogeneous pool, allow explicit partitions for exceptions; joining offers never rewrites receipt costs or historical order bindings. Unknown mappings go to review before publication.

### Quantities at a committed revision

| Quantity | Rule |
|---|---|
| O, locally on hand | Sum of physically present units, including inspected stock, receiving/quarantine, and goods in a pick tote or packed parcel awaiting handoff. Exclude shipments handed over, disposal and missing pieces removed by an audited count. |
| U, unavailable | Physically present units explicitly blocked from sale (unidentified, damaged, quarantine, awaiting inspection). Each unit has exactly one stock state. |
| R, reserved | Physical backing for open order commitments and authorized internal/build holds, including picked/packed units still on hand. Distinguish unassigned pool commitment from assignment to a location. Assigning, picking or packing an already backed unit never reserves it again. |
| F, available to sell | O − U − R. O, U, R, F are nonnegative and O = U + R + F for each compatible pool. Reservation and quarantine cannot claim the same unit. Record unmet obligations separately as positive shortage S. Never use negative stock to balance the books. |
| D, desired online | At the revision in question, integer quantity assigned for public sale per active offer, after caps, withheld units and bulk-sale policy. Across offers sharing a pool, sum(D) ≤ F. Inactive/stockroom goods have zero public exposure even when the provider reports stock there. Show the target stockroom quantity separately. Block replenishment for unresolved demand, unknown identity or a stale sales feed. |
| Awaiting publication | Physical stock may be eligible yet held back by policy (F − sum(D)). For a bound offer show D, last observed public quantity B with timestamp and stockroom state, pending local changes and reasons separately. A positive D−B is a difference to explain, never a dispatch authorization. A new unsent offer has no B. |
| Confirmed and uncertain | A confirmed operation has endpoint-appropriate success evidence, authoritative read-back and matched effects at a recorded observation frontier. An inconclusive dispatch remains uncertain. B may be unknown, stale or superseded by a later sale. Preserve a historical confirmation even when the current state later diverges. |

Record a witnessed count against its specific receipt position and confirmed bin. The observation records the compared ledger revision and quantity. A lower count opens a physical shortage for review and fences new backing and transfers from that position. An equal or higher recount supplies evidence while the open case stays under review. When a later witness matches the current physical ledger for that receipt position, the operator can confirm the registered bin and reason to close the case. The resolution retains both witnesses, the physical quantity, quarantine, holds, and draft backing. [Exact recount resolution](https://github.com/abrianbaker80/BrickVault-V2/issues/37) A surplus is unidentified and needs source and condition evidence before receiving or release.

Under D09, distinguish the observation from an authorized stock adjustment and from an owner decision to release or reassign a hold. A posted reduction removes only the confirmed missing units. When a required unit loses physical backing, retain the obligation and its unbacked remainder. Existing internal holds retain their identities and priority until the owner chooses a specific hold and quantity to release or reassign. A customer obligation has priority over proposed new internal holds. Keep any unresolved amount visible and never cancel a buyer obligation as a side effect. For an open line, effective required units = shipped units + remaining physically backed units + unbacked shortage (net of explicit cancellation/amendment). Guard over-shipments caused by amendments with a conflict and a return or adjustment case. Track historical quantities separately from amounts outstanding. On carrier handoff reduce O and R by the same backed quantity. F stays constant. A received return enters quarantine; only an explicit inspection can move it back to sellable stock. Crediting or refunding money is a separate fact.

Under D10, a proposed stock loss shows how much unpublished offer backing the selected position can still support after project reservations. The operator explicitly assigns the required reduction across the offers that draw from that position. Confirming the loss records one transaction and receipt. The selected bin, witness, before and after quantities, costs and individual offer decisions remain traceable. A release from a project hold has its own receipt. [Stock count and loss decisions](https://github.com/abrianbaker80/BrickVault-V2/issues/22)

Under D11, when the counted position contains quarantined pieces, the operator confirms which quarantine records lost pieces. Confirming zero is an explicit choice. The remaining unavailable quantity equals the opening quarantine quantity less the chosen loss from those records. The remaining pieces can support offers only after deducting this unavailable quantity and existing reservations from the witnessed physical count. The loss receipt preserves the selected records and quantities. An inspection release keeps its own receipt. [Quarantined stock loss](https://github.com/abrianbaker80/BrickVault-V2/issues/33#issuecomment-7155121127)

## Proposed relational design

| Aggregate and relationships | Fields and rules that matter |
|---|---|
| CatalogItem, Color, CatalogMapping, Variant | Stable internal IDs, external identities/aliases, typed dimensions, normalized condition/variant and per-type validation. Version alias changes. Unknown identities cannot enter an offer. |
| Source, Purchase, CostComponent, Batch, ReceiptLine, CostLayer | Purchase → component breakdown and one or more receiving batches → identified quantities. Record supplier/source, date, currency and original amount; retain freight, discounts and other acquisition components. A receipt can remain unclassified. A separate split/part-out transformation consumes inputs and creates outputs once, with conserved allocated cost or an explicit unallocated residual. |
| Location, StockPosition, Movement, MovementLeg | Stable location ID, unique active scannable code and label aliases. Version position balances. Movement header includes intent ID, reason, timestamp, actor, links; legs carry layer, variant, location, state, units. A transfer balances to zero per layer across internal positions. Receipts, dispatch, loss and return use explicit boundaries; record corrections as counter-entries. No deletion of referenced locations. |
| CountObservation, PhysicalShortageCase | Append the witnessed quantity, reason, registered bin confirmation, receipt position, compared ledger quantity and purchase revision. Retain one open case per affected position and its opening evidence. A later count adds an observation. A posted correction and an explicit owner allocation require their own command receipts. |
| Obligation, Reservation, PlacementAllocation, PickTask | Order demand or internal hold, backed amount, shortage and handling state. On assignment, move backing from pool to a position once. Picked/packed quantities are substates of reservations. One placement cannot back two pieces of demand with the same unit. Preserve release and replacement history. |
| Offer, OfferStockShare, RemoteLotBinding, DesiredRevision, RemoteObservation | Maturity, price/attribute revisions, exposure cap and assigned supply. Historical bindings resolve old order lines after merge, disappearance or replacement. Observations carry store, time, run, completeness and fingerprint. |
| PriceDecision, RuleVersion, PriceObservation | Decimal prices, currency, condition, observation period, evidence freshness, rounding policy, owner overrides with expiry/priority and approval. Keep financial cost and provider my_cost separate. Preview bulk changes at fixed offer revisions and surface skipped rows with reasons. |
| MarketplaceOrder, OrderRevision, OrderLineRevision, DemandEffect | Unique (store, provider order ID). Retain source status separately from local handling stage and payment state. Canonical membership/quantities by stable source identity where verified; internal line IDs with source evidence, revisions and batch membership. Financial fields store original currency and decimal scale. |
| PickWave/Tote, PickEvent, PackEvent, Parcel, ShipmentLine, Tracking, ReturnCase | One order may have several parcels. Every shipped unit traces to allocation and layer. Packing requires the applicable pick and verification evidence. Reprints do not create shipments. Preserve physical handoff evidence, refund decision and later remote status/receipt independently. Wave is optional. |
| CommandReceipt, SyncIntent/Attempt, InboxHint, SyncRun, OperationReceipt, DriftCase | Unique local command ID and canonical payload hash; same ID/different payload is a conflict. Store local effects, request fingerprint, prior observation, expected effect, attempts, claim/fence, readbacks, outcomes and operator interventions. A notification or API response alone cannot close unrelated rows. |
| MigrationMap/Exception, AuditEvent, Backup verification | Unique (stable source installation, entity, legacy ID) across runs, snapshot fingerprints, approved disposition and deterministic target IDs. Append to a restricted audit trail. Redact/exclude personal data and credentials from business-event metadata, logs, metrics, fixtures and screenshots. |

**Integrity boundaries.** Use PostgreSQL identity keys or UUIDs, foreign keys, unique store/order and live lot/order-effect keys, check constraints, decimal types, UTC timestamps and explicit currencies. Precision proposal: NUMERIC(20,6) for unit price/cost and internal allocations, higher precision during division; preserve a source value with extra precision and flag it before rounding. Currency-specific minor units for actual settlements. Fix deterministic rounding/residual rules per allocation document. Keep lines of a balanced movement, position projection, reservation, local receipt and any resulting outbox intents in one DB transaction. Cross-row conservation requires a transaction under locks; a CHECK on one row cannot enforce it. Reject direct business-table edits from the UI. Use least-privilege service and separate migration credentials.

Lock affected offers/pools/positions and order rows in a global, stable order. Recheck revisions and available units under lock; on local serialization failure retry the complete DB-only command with the same ID. Use partial unique indexes for active bindings, single in-flight remote dispatch per offer, allocations/revisions/operation keys as appropriate. Index variant/location availability, order status + time, active allocation by position, store/remote lot, pending job priority + next time, observation generation and movement by position + monotonic sequence. Verify query plans with snapshot-scale and larger fixture data. A background checker recomputes balances, backed demand, offer partitions and outbox coverage; discrepancies freeze affected actions and retain evidence.

**Why a ledger and outbox.** A current balance alone cannot explain a shortage, a cost layer consumed or a reversal. A ledger plus small in-transaction projections makes those answers reconstructible without replaying every read. One worker in the same deployable polls a PostgreSQL outbox; intent committed with a local action survives a crash between the commit and network I/O. Cost is extra rows, contention, retention and restore testing. One transactional database suffices. Rebuild derived balances from ledger + approved opening entries in an isolated replay and compare. Do not retroactively present pre-cutover opening balances as a complete historical ledger. [PostgreSQL locking](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/explicit-locking.html)

### Inbound ingestion

1. Maintain separate completed discovery runs for received filed/unfiled lists. Record scope, start/end, status, observed IDs and any limits. The design evidence does not establish a reliable modified-since cursor, paging contract or complete change feed. Verify coverage against the seller UI during authorized qualification. Incomplete runs cannot infer removal. Sweep open orders and a rotating historical set even when summaries are identical; item swaps can leave counts and totals unchanged. On outage recovery replay discovery including filed and known IDs before any increase in exposure. Define coverage horizon from provider retention and measured volumes; keep a manual exception lane for missing/purged history.
2. A notification inserts a deduplicated hint if there is a stable ID, otherwise coalesces a fetch by order ID. Treat callbacks as untrusted hints. Fetch canonical details and *all* item batches; record a deterministic normalized snapshot, optionally fetch twice across inconsistent header/items. Reject partial groups, mixed currency without supported handling, unknown mandatory state, identity mismatch and malformed responses. Mark observations in UTC; a remote timestamp by itself cannot reliably order item revisions.
3. Serialize reads/apply per order or give them generations so a late worker cannot replace a newer accepted fetch. Apply an older-looking or inconsistent snapshot only after fresh refetch/review. Provider changes A→B→A legitimately repeat earlier content. Compare to the current accepted revision, use a new monotonic revision for each actual transition, and key effects by order + applied transition + semantic line. A unique hash of all historical content would suppress a legitimate reversal.
4. Prefer a documented stable line ID when actually present; otherwise group equivalent order demand by remote lot, sale attributes/price and verified batch identity when stable. Preserve multiplicity, raw batch grouping in the restricted evidence, and permutations. Never use list offset as a lasting ID. If equivalent lines reorder, demand stays constant; if history/price/allocation makes assignment ambiguous, block fulfillment of affected lines and expose the difference. A missing lot opens a mapping/shortage case and fences relevant listing increases.
5. On import, atomically apply the new **difference in obligation**. Reserve backing once, record any shortage and local offer target, and tag quantity change as provider-originated sale (already reflected remotely when verified). No second outbound decrement for the sale. On order amendments release only eligible units, add obligations for increases, invalidate stale pick instructions and require physical recovery for packed goods. A cancellation may auto-restock remotely depending on provider settings/workflow. Observe that outcome before reconciling; isolate remote excess until physical stock is verified. Partial shipments/returns, terminal states and remote-only changes retain independent records.

### Outbound publication and recovery

1. Each physical/price/policy command carries a stable client command ID, expected revision and canonical intent. Commit business facts plus uniquely keyed publication intent together. Each dispatch gets a separate attempt ID. Store operation class (create, local signed quantity change, price/attributes, move stockroom, retire/delete, tracking, status), store/lot/offer, revision, relevant old/new values, known local cause, consumed effects and before-image. A local ID offers no provider-side dedup guarantee.
2. Coalesce or supersede *undispatched* revisions under a lock, retaining their lineage. For dispatched revisions queue successors behind the unresolved attempt. Worker leases include a fencing token. Record dispatching and commit **before** sending. Expired lease after possible send means outcome unknown; a second worker cannot redispatch the same operation. Fencing controls V2 processes only. During failover ensure the previous process/transport cannot still send before accepting a new dispatcher.
3. Poll orders and refresh the exact listing before a risky publication. Compare full identity, active/stockroom state, relevant attributes and known changes with the expected baseline. For quantity, send only the net **explained, unreflected local change**. A discrepancy from a stale cache, a sale or an unexplained provider edit cannot create a positive publication. Recheck current target and remaining authorization before sending. Avoid mixing changes to quantity and price unless the endpoint is qualified for that combination. Submit one lot per operation initially.
4. Record transport/meta and returned resource or recorded no-content receipt. Read fresh lot/detail and relevant order updates. Match actual quantity to the documented local delta **adjusted for evidenced concurrent events**, and verify only the intended attributes changed. If readback fails, remain sent/awaiting confirmation. If desired already holds but attribution is impossible, label it observed converged, retain the ambiguous attempt and never count an acknowledged mutation twice.
5. Classify pre-send validation/cancellation, definitive rejection without execution, throttling with conclusive no-apply semantics, possibly sent, confirmed, observed convergence, blocked and superseded separately. Delay retry of proven safe operations, honor Retry-After when supplied. Timeout, dropped response, ambiguous 5xx, redirect/HTML after a write, process death and partial response after sending require reconciliation. Even a client timeout can precede late application. Repeated GET matching the pre-state cannot prove the request will never apply. Freeze the affected write lane until evidence resolves it or an owner records an explicit corrective disposition with a new operation identity. Preserve all attempts.
6. Create uses narrowly defined identity, allowed consolidation behavior, preflight list and returned ID plus detail. A timeout followed by one similar lot may be the old lot consolidated, a new one or an unrelated listing. Do not repeat POST or adopt an ambiguous match. For retire/delete, confirm exact old binding/attributes and impact of open orders, prefer a reviewed withdraw/retain operation where justified, and verify via full inventory including stockroom. Do not reuse a retired ID mapping or infer deletion solely from a missing read. No autonomous mass delete, duplicate cleanup or rollback deltas.
7. Tracking and status are individually resumable. Preserve every parcel and tracking number internally. When the provider exposes only an order-level tracking field, never overwrite a previous parcel silently. Keep the order-level shipping transition pending until all non-canceled units meet the qualified marketplace transition and any partial-parcel handling is explicitly supported or reviewed. Example: tracking read-back matches, status fails → finish only outstanding status after verifying physical handoff and permitted transition. An external SHIPPED flag without local physical evidence opens a case. Reconcile price/tier/remarks edits independently; never overwrite an unknown operator edit during quantity repair.

**Synthetic race example.** A verified lot has 10; V2 receives 3 and dispatches +3. BrickLink sells 2 before the confirmation read; observed value is 11. Once the order is confirmed, V2 holds O=13, R=2, F=11. If evidence establishes both effects, acknowledge +3 once. Sending another +2 to reach the old target of 13 would oversell. If the response to +3 was lost and a manual +3 or an unrelated offsetting sale could explain the same result, show the actual exposure and leave attribution unresolved. If another local receipt arrives meanwhile it has a separate revision and waits behind the first.

### Scheduling, reconciliation and operator controls

A synthetic request-budget example uses a 5-minute order discovery cadence (two list scopes gives 576 calls/day before detail), fetch detail/items only on hints, new orders or scheduled rechecks; separately reserve capacity for rotating complete detail checks. Example: 50 full two-read detail checks/day adds 100 calls. Schedule low-priority catalog/pricing behind order and confirmation reserves. Tune to the actual account, shared V1/shadow traffic and observed order latency. Persist a conservative rolling 24-hour limiter plus the verified provider-day limit across restarts; track every actual attempt, conservative headroom and capacity reserved for orders. Use targeted detail reads between complete sweeps. Periodic full inventory list scans plus targeted lot lookups, explicit complete vs partial pass, monotonically fenced runs. Limit concurrency, exponential backoff + jitter, shared cooldown and a circuit breaker. Resume with a read-only catch-up, then release safe work in priority order. Show oldest unprocessed order, last complete sweep per scope, listing staleness, ready/in-flight/uncertain counts, oldest backlog, budget/headroom and the age of any blocked case.

Classify differences as expected sale/order revision, known pending receipt/withdrawal, price or attribute edit, new remote-only lot, unbound local stock, stockroom/retain transition, ambiguous create/delete, or unexplained difference. Each case shows last confirmed baseline, current physical/held/free quantities, local decisions after baseline, order evidence, current read timestamp, remote ID/terms, suggested actions and risk. Allow refresh, identify/rebind with evidence, recount, accept a deliberate remote change into a **new local decision** or authorize a narrowly scoped corrective operation. Retain previous and new values and reason. Missing or incomplete data never implies zero. No “make all match” overwrite.

When BrickLink is down, V2 can safely record receipts, backed allocations for known orders, custody changes and local journals. It cannot know current remote exposure, stop remote sales, prove external delivery or promise a worldwide atomic stock count. Offline workstation input during loss of the authoritative server is read-only/stale; do not accept independent stock writes at the client. Unknown demand plus physical shortages require an operator to consider a marketplace-side sales pause after connectivity returns. No architecture can guarantee zero oversells across a disconnected storefront without provider cooperation.

### Binding edge cases and additional controls

* **A canceled pick is still in a tote.** Release an untouched bin commitment R→F only when the units are in their verified sellable position. In the cancellation transaction transfer picked/packed backing R→U (recovery), preserving layer, quantity, parcel/tote and actual position. O and F are unchanged for these units. Close outstanding shortage for a canceled, previously unbacked unit without freeing any stock. Scan return to bin/inspection before U→F. A remote restock may create excess exposure immediately. Open a high-priority exception and reconcile the actual remote effect. The seller’s removal workflow can also alter totals, dates, payment and status. Compare the complete order revision. [Cancellation](https://www.bricklink.com/help.asp?helpID=73&viewType=shop) · [Item removal](https://www.bricklink.com/helpLang.asp?helpID=267)
* **A POST can affect another offer.** Serialize create/rebind/consolidation by store and verified provider matching group, including all candidate bound lots, across all publication paths. Use a store-wide fence for affected writes when that matching scope is unknown. Persist the fence through any uncertain interval. Refresh each affected binding and full relevant attribute set. A human review or new operation ID cannot prove an earlier delayed request will never execute. While late execution remains plausible, retain the fence across replacement workers and corrective writes. Only conclusive settlement/non-application or a separately approved, bounded recovery accounting for both outcomes can release it. Test simultaneous creates, process suspension followed by late completion, and manual edits matching the expected delta.
* **Per-offer supply.** Require 0 ≤ Dᵢ ≤ free shareᵢ; the sum of disjoint free shares cannot exceed F. Unassigned supply is explicit. Reassignment is a versioned local business event. Compare submitted local movement IDs, acknowledged provider changes and order event evidence. Equal current numbers alone cannot attribute an ambiguous operation.
* **Evidence has a horizon.** Exactly-once applies to accepted observable transitions. If A→B→A occurs between observations or an order disappears outside the provider retention window, current state may reconcile while the intermediate events are unknowable. Keep a coverage exception when a missing interval cannot be reconstructed.
* **Money must also conserve.** Per purchase/currency, total acquisition components equal assigned layer totals plus explicitly unallocated residual. Preserve the extended amount in addition to a display unit price. Every partial consumption splits original value between remaining and issued units. Allocate rounding residual deterministically to the last remaining unit or tranche. Reverse the original shipped layer cost on a traceable accepted return. Record unknown cost, refunds/fees and dated FX separately; no invented exchange rate or historical gross margin.
* **Provider contract details.** The indexed V3 error table lists 200/201/204 and request, authorization, resource, validation and server errors; no dedicated partial-success code or row-level bulk receipt was established. The published create reference does not establish a consolidation switch or default. Prefer single create with a full before/after binding check. Never recycle a retired provider lot ID into another local history. [API errors](https://www.bricklink.com/v3/api.page?page=error-handling) · [Create inventory](https://www.bricklink.com/v3/api.page?page=create-inventory) · [Seller lot guidance](https://www.bricklink.com/help.asp?helpID=2699)
* **Provider information lifecycle.** The indexed terms distinguish display freshness (six hours for item/product information or images and 24 hours for other BrickLink content relative to the website) from reasonable retention. Verify wording and applicability to this private app at M4. Refresh or suppress affected content when it cannot be displayed within the terms. Provide owner/contact, privacy/consent and attribution where required; minimize access to buyer information, handle authorized erasure, and expire raw material and old backups on an approved schedule. Preserve lawful seller-owned financial and stock evidence separately. [API terms](https://www.bricklink.com/helpLang.asp?helpID=2216&viewType=shop)
