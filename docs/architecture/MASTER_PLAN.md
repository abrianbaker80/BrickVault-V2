# BrickVault V2 master plan

The owner accepted M0 and M1 and approved a public source repository with fresh history. The deployed application, store data and private supporting records remain restricted. D01–D07 define the approved direction. Release scope remains provisional under D03. M2–M9 require separate authorization.

## Recommendation

The proposed operations system has an always-on application and database that continue order discovery when an operator closes the browser. One backend owns stock and order changes, and one qualified publisher sends BrickLink changes. Use a narrowly scoped device connector only for an approved workflow the browser cannot complete. Hosting requirements remain subject to the D04 review.

The unit of work is the piece the owner receives, finds, prices, picks and ships. A single offer can represent several physically separate acquisition layers; an order reduces what is available to sell immediately, even while its pieces are still in the drawer. A failed network reply may describe a write that actually succeeded. The architecture must account for both facts before it can safely publish another quantity.

**Approved direction.** Reserve stock on authoritative order obligations and retain custody through carrier handoff. Use a single qualified automatic marketplace publisher and preserve traceable acquisition costs. The owner selected Workbench navigation and visual design and Vue 3 with TypeScript for the frontend. The stack still requires transaction, dependency, hosting, restore and compatibility proof at the applicable milestone. [Decision register](DECISIONS.md)

## One complete operating day

| Task | Operator workflow | Durable result and exception path |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase and receive | Record source, purchase date/currency, actual price, freight/discounts and evidence. Open one or more batches. Receive quantities incrementally. | Posted purchase costs separate from unallocated spend. Duplicate receipt replays once; an unknown cost or source is explicit. |
| Identify, sort and store | Find a catalog item by typed number/name or verified scanner input; confirm color, condition, completeness and description. Confirm receiving layer and bin, split among bins if necessary. | Per-location movement and traceable acquisition layer. Unidentified/damaged or incomplete items remain held. A suggestion or camera guess cannot create sellable units. |
| Offer and publish | Choose an existing compatible listing or separate offer, set quantity policy, price, attributes, bulk/tier, description and stockroom. Preview named rows plus expected effect. | Durable intent and fresh marketplace evidence. Unbound/ambiguous lots, missing cost under cost-dependent pricing, stale source, or uncertain previous write get an actionable reason. |
| Sell and allocate | Import BrickLink orders, amendments, status and financials. Auto-back unambiguous demand and present shortages/stale mappings immediately. | Reservations reduce sellable quantity once. Substitution of a different condition or variant requires explicit buyer/marketplace resolution; keep attempted substitution in the audit. |
| Pick | Start with one order. Route by physical location/pick sequence. Show image when permitted/fresh, item/color/condition, bin, quantity and tote. Scan or confirm source/tote and units. Reopen at the last verified step. | Partial picks retain their remaining tasks. Wrong bin/tote, amended line, occupied hold or shortage blocks progression of affected units; safe lines can proceed. |
| Pack and ship | Verify actual contents against the tote and parcel; record partial parcels and remaining lines. Prepare address/picking slips, access printable documents/labels, enter carrier and tracking, and record handoff. | A printed label or closed box leaves goods in custody. Handoff consumes the physical units and their cost layer; tracking and remote status have separate receipts. Reprint is a distinct action. |
| Cancel, return and reconcile | Show local custody, remote order revision, possible provider restock, refund state and physical return path. Record received returns into inspection, then reshelve or write off. | No second marketplace credit for an automatic restock, double refund or automatic relist of a piece still in a tote. Audited issue resolution explains both sides. |
| Measure profitability | Report purchase/batch recovery, realized line/order margins and remaining cost layers by currency and period. Export controls and drill into each number. | Net item revenue + seller-retained shipping/other income − refunds − attributed cost of dispatched goods − actual fees/postage/other attributable expenses. Exclude marketplace-collected pass-through taxes and avoid double-counting discounts or settled fees. Separate actuals, estimated expenses/FX and unknown costs; label partial coverage. |

Keep order-first daily navigation. Purchasing, storage, pricing and sync each change whether an order can be fulfilled. Release requires the complete sale-to-shipment loop. New storefront, POS, coupon campaigns and general multi-marketplace orchestration add little to this single-store model. Batch picking may save walking but introduces tote cross-contamination; qualify the single-order route first. Separate customer sales from owner movements/build reservations. A unified stock model must honor real holds even if an ancillary UI moves beyond launch. [Feature coverage](FEATURE_MATRIX.md) · [Bricqer decisions](Bricqer-guide-review.md)

## Architecture and workstation behavior

Before provisioning any V2 server, measure Proxmox CPU/RAM/storage headroom and contention, document network/DNS/TLS and fixed provider egress, isolation and secrets, independent backup/restore, power/storage/hosting costs and owner approval. M0 provisions no guest.

The browser uses authenticated, same-origin typed HTTPS APIs. Commands carry a stable request ID, expected business revision and precise target IDs; reads carry scope, as-of time and completeness. The server contains Catalog/Receiving, Stock/Cost, Orders/Fulfillment, Pricing and Marketplace modules. Only stock transactions move units. The BrickLink adapter owns OAuth, protocol interpretation, observations, budget and external receipts. The worker and HTTP module share transactional state. An order notification is an optional hint. Polling remains operational without exposing any public callback endpoint.

Limit the private app to a private network/VPN with TLS, one owner identity (passkey or independently authenticated private reverse proxy), session expiry, CSRF/origin checks and separate device credentials. No browser or hardware utility gets direct DB or BrickLink credentials. Keep authenticated production and simulator endpoints segregated by environment, deployment and secret access; never rely on a UI switch to protect production. Log correlation IDs and redacted errors. Restricted order/shipping fields expire under a reviewed policy. Backup keys and restore credentials require an independent recovery path.

**Device boundary.** Start with named scan input fields accepting HID wedge or typed identifiers, printable PDF with dimensions, and operator-confirmed carrier tracking. Exact copies of purchase invoices or labels with addresses have restricted access. For direct thermal print/serial scale/camera, a paired local agent initiates authenticated outbound connections to the server and accepts only enumerated device verbs/jobs for that workstation. Bind printer selection explicitly; camera data transfer requires operator action. Never expose a general shell or arbitrary file/print target. Run as least-privilege user/session where drivers need desktop access, supervise startup and version handshake, show offline status. Serial jobs keep event IDs and device IDs. A spool acknowledgement proves submission; paper/contents/scan legibility require a witnessed physical test. An uncertain print requires inspection and a deliberate reprint to avoid duplicate labels. No carrier label purchase via an unverified integration. Use an operator-obtained carrier label with print/import and tracking until a carrier API is separately approved. Scale, specialized camera, recognition and sorter actuation have separate scope gates. Qualify ordinary browser tasks independently on every supported client. Bind each hardware job to the workstation that owns the device.

## Workbench visual design

The provided BrickVault logo shows a yellow brick built into a graphite vault on a warm off-white field. Use warm white and graphite as working surfaces; reserve yellow with dark text for the primary action or current step. Use green/amber/red with an icon and explicit text for confirmed/pending/problem. Price, totals and quantity columns use tabular numerals. Start with a legible UI family such as Inter and an emphasized weight for task titles. Explore light first, high-contrast/OS preference and a dark theme as justified. Keep dense tables readable and use visible keyboard focus.

| Workspace | Interaction requirements |
|---|---|
| Today and Orders | Open picks, shortages, late orders and sync conflicts link to the actual task. Order detail prioritizes order items, then allocations/bins, tote/parcel and timeline. Keep local stage, remote order status and payment separate. |
| Stock | Search all item types, filter condition/location/batch/stock state, inspect a row without selecting it for action. Inspector shows complete placements, provenance, free/held/desired/observed and timestamps. Selected IDs, whole-filter result and all-inventory actions show explicit scope, revision and impact before commit. |
| Receiving | Progressive entry with tab order, persistent batch context, scan result and confirm location; undo creates a traced reversal. Preview subsequent price/publication and identify what is blocked. |
| Picking and packing | One task row gets large count and input focus, tote/parcel stays visible. Support keyboard-only work, wrong/duplicate scans, short count, restart and amended order. Small windows show sequential pages with maintained context. |
| Sync and reporting | Present the local cause, remote observation age, suspected sale and remaining uncertainty side-by-side. Every alert has an action and scope. Profit details distinguish actual, estimated, unallocated and missing amounts. |

M1 compared Workbench and Ledger through the same fictional workflows. The React and Vue inventory registers shared data, design constraints and interaction tests. The review covers usability, visual quality, maintainability, performance, accessibility and development efficiency. Workbench keeps navigation visible in a graphite side rail and fits one more complete lot at each tested desktop width. Its inspector opens in a dialog when register space is limited. Both framework implementations passed the same tasks. [M1 review and measurements](../../prototypes/m1/REVIEW.md)

Carry the accepted interactions into production qualification. Check long names and variants, split placements, shortage, ambiguous sends, pending print and returns. Distinguish failed, empty, partial, stale and confirmed-zero reads. Verify the intended desktop viewport, 1280×720, a narrow 900-pixel window, 150% scale, keyboard focus and dialog focus restoration, screen-reader table and row actions, and status labels understandable without color. Production screens require real integration, render checks and owner acceptance of material differences.

## Migration and one controlled cutover

1. **Inventory the sources on isolated snapshots.** Fingerprint schema, version, source DB, snapshot ID/time, constraints, table-level counts, units and currencies. Include branch-specific tables, V1 client journals and preferences, retained operation receipts, remote snapshots and approved backup/config manifests. Capture manifests outside Git without secrets/PII. Independently restore and verify both the DB and the files that a full recovery needs.
2. **Translate once, repeatably.** Versioned offline importer with immutable source, staged typed input, crosswalk and row-level disposition. Mappings use stable source installation/table/PK identities across rehearsals; attach snapshot ID, checksum and importer version as evidence, not as a reason to assign a different business ID at each snapshot. Stage to a disposable target and promote only after checks pass. Repeat same input twice and compare deterministic IDs, movements, control totals and exceptions. Later snapshots expressly supersede staged runs. Never replay V1 jobs into a live writer.
3. **Map actual semantics.** Typed item number/type, local vs BrickLink color, N/U and legacy condition/completeness, private/public text, catalog aliases, primary/all placements and stable location codes. Preserve remote inventory IDs, order direction, dates, line prices/currencies, allocations and build holds. Map internal vs public vs stockroom state. Use documented opening entries for physical custody and open obligations. V1 may deduct at packing; for any packed-but-unshipped parcel establish physical custody and explain the bridging entry before V2’s handoff rule starts. Proof at order/parcel level prevents a second deduction. For already shipped items import historical outcome/COGS coverage without issuing opening stock.
4. **Handle ambiguity.** Weighted legacy values stay labeled as weighted; an unknown source/cost/location stays unknown or in a quarantined exception. Never fabricate a purchase to satisfy a foreign key. Map historic order lines with stable local IDs plus preserved lot evidence; no invented provider line IDs. Record archived V1 history and non-core records with a retrievable reference. Explicitly classify every pending minifigure record by custody/sellability. Outbox rows, Flutter journals, unknown sends, fulfillment steps and reads get a side-by-side disposition: settled with evidence, genuinely unsent and newly authorized once, or fenced for review. Preserve before/after values and signed exceptions.
5. **Reconcile by independent controls.** Counts crosswalk by entity with splits/merges explained, units by type/variant/condition/lot/location, active holds, unassigned shortages, picked/packed/on-hand and remote bindings including zero-retained. Reconcile purchase components, unit precision, known value by currency, outstanding unallocated cost, order revenue/refunds/fees and unknown-coverage counts; test per-layer rounding. Verify ledger vs projection and produced exports vs source controls. Rehearse at least two snapshots (including production-shaped exceptional fixtures); record timing, residuals and hard-stop conditions. Reject negative free stock, duplicate bindings, orphaned open demands, unexplained unit/money differences or a nondeterministic rerun.
6. **Shadow without a second writer.** With separate authorization and budget, read marketplace inventory and both order scopes, compare V2 with V1 and classify discrepancies. No V2 publisher credentials in dry-run environments. Measure order lag, odds of incomplete scans, restore/recovery time and required downtime. Agree on store-sale handling during the window. Even when sales may be paused, prove catch-up across the boundary.
7. **Freeze and establish the authority point.** the owner approves the specific window and plan. Record T0, last verified order/lot frontier and all in-flight requests. Quiesce V1 inventory/order mutations, all workers, cron jobs, direct cleanup/reprice/fulfillment publishers, manual workstation actions and any other external writer. Verify process/egress fencing and drains, including uncertain previously dispatched writes; stopping a service cannot erase an in-flight request. Fence unresolved lots. Take final consistent source + journals + backup manifest, restore-verify, then run final translation and controls. Keep store order intake separately accounted for while V1 is frozen.
8. **Catch up before publishing.** With V2 publisher still disabled, fetch received orders including filed/unfiled and changed open orders covering before and after T0, and a complete fresh listing inventory. Resolve overlap using historical bindings, existing demand and effect receipts; never reserve the same sale twice. Loop until two coherent comparison passes or otherwise evidenced stable frontier; do not assume the live storefront stopped changing. No unexplained enabled-lot drift, open demand, conflicting writer or unclassified sent attempt. Explicitly list fenced exceptions and their sale exposure for the owner. For unsafe exposure, obtain and verify sale withdrawal/closure or postpone cutover.
9. **Qualify and switch.** Separate owner approval specifies store, maximum action/lot/units, monitoring and abort criteria. Use a sanctioned test store if available; otherwise a bounded production canary only during sole-writer fencing. Read back each effect and reconcile new orders, then enable only individually qualified operations. Observe an ordinary complete sale and fulfillment, compare reservations, actual handoff, price/quantity and remote status. Review each shift initially, including orders placed during freeze. Record first V2 writer epoch, last V1 writer, lag, unresolved cases and owner acceptance.
10. **Back out against reality.** Only when there have been no post-T0 V2 business effects (including receipts, reservations, picks, financial decisions or remote sends), stop V2, recheck fresh market observations and bring V1 forward through missed orders before authorizing V1 as sole writer. If any post-T0 V2 business effect or possibly sent operation exists, disable/fence V2 and preserve its ledger, receipts, physical picks, shipments, edits and buyer activity. Prefer forward recovery. To return to V1, reconstruct and validate each post-T0 change into an isolated V1 recovery target, reconcile fresh market and physical custody, drain uncertain requests and obtain owner approval before releasing a single V1 writer. Never restore an old DB over live sales or issue inverse deltas blindly. A material unexplained difference keeps writers fenced. Rehearse both branches with new sales, a parcel handoff and a lost write response.

## Risks requiring a decision or live verification

| Risk | Consequence and gate |
|---|---|
| Unknown historical costs and blended layers | A blended opening balance can lack the source evidence for exact historical margin. Preserve unknown and coverage or obtain genuine source documents. Apply the approved D05 rule and refer consequential exceptions to the owner. |
| Missing provider event/line identity or unavailable history | Polling recovers visible states; previously purged or transient revisions might be unobservable. Verify full seller listing/order coverage, repeated batches, restock and term limits with current vendor evidence. Block unexplained differences. |
| Provider uncertainty and no established test seller | No honest zero-impact proof of a real stock write without a sanctioned test context. M4 exercises isolated faults, M8 reconciles shadow, M9 needs separate contained live approval. |
| Restore or rollback while remote orders continue | Restoring a DB can resurrect a dispatch or forget a sale. Require restore fencing, order discovery and inventory reconciliation before releasing a publisher. |
| Local device behavior and carrier choices | USB/camera/scale and label stock can differ from installed drivers. Confirm the owner's essential devices, label formats and carrier workflow before final scope, then test physically. |
| Physical custody at transition and ancillary V1 workflows | Recount/review designated high-risk lots and any active pick/packed parcel. Approve a retention or replacement plan for every used capability before retirement. |
| Repository protection and project-scoped orchestration | Verify enforceable rules and a successful required check on the public repository. Witness the full Insiders sidebar delegation, correction, review and approval-pause exercise. |
